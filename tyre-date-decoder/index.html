Tyre Date Code Decoder

Buying tyres and want to check they are not old stock? Every tyre sidewall hides its birth date in the DOT code. Decode it here.

Where to find it

Look for the letters DOT on the sidewall, followed by a string of letters and numbers. The last four digits are the date code: the first two are the week (01-53), the last two are the year. Example: 2625 = week 26 of 2025. Some tyres have the full DOT code on only one side - check both sidewalls (on a car, that may mean turning the wheel or looking from under the vehicle).

What age is acceptable?

Rubber ages even on the shelf and on the vehicle. There is no law fixing a retail "expiry" - condition and storage history matter alongside the date. For tyres in use, Michelin's published guidance is a professional inspection at least once a year from 5 years old, and replacement by 10 years from the sidewall date as a precaution - whatever the tread looks like, spare included. Other tyre and vehicle makers publish their own limits, some stricter - your maker's own guidance wins, and cracks, bulges, puncture damage or worn tread mean replace now, whatever the age. Check the date on all four tyres and the spare before paying, and match it against the bill date.

Runs entirely in your browser. The WWYY format is the international DOT date convention used by all major tyre brands sold in India; it is not a legal standard.
