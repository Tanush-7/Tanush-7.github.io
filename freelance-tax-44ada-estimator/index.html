Freelancer Presumptive Tax Estimator

Freelancing and lost on taxes? Work out presumptive income (the old "44ADA", now Section 58 of the Income-tax Act 2025) and when advance tax is due.

Before you start

This is an estimator, not filing advice. The Income-tax Act 2025 applies from tax year 2026-27; the presumptive scheme for professionals (50% of receipts) lives in Section 58(2) of the new Act - it was Section 44ADA of the 1961 Act. Whether you qualify, which regime suits you, and what you finally owe depend on your full-year picture - confirm on incometax.gov.in or with a CA before you file. Foreign-client income, GST registration and TDS credits each have their own rules not covered here.

1. Presumptive income - Section 58(2), professionals

The scheme declares 50% of gross receipts as income (or your actual profit if higher). Eligibility: resident individuals and non-LLP firms in specified professions, with receipts up to Rs 50 lakh - or Rs 75 lakh when cash receipts stay within 5%. Declaring less than 50% removes the benefit and triggers books and audit if your income is above the basic exemption.

The rules this estimator applies
RuleWhat the law says
Presumptive incomeSection 58(2) of the Income-tax Act 2025: for specified professionals (Table Sl. No. 3, the old 44ADA), deemed income is 50% of gross receipts - or actual profit if higher. Receipts ceiling Rs 50 lakh / Rs 75 lakh (cash within 5%).
Advance tax - presumptiveIf you declare under the presumptive scheme, Section 408(2) lets you pay the whole year's advance tax in one instalment by 15 March - no quarterly instalments needed.
Advance tax - regularNot declaring presumptively? Section 408(1): four instalments - 15% by 15 Jun, 45% (cumulative) by 15 Sep, 75% by 15 Dec, 100% by 15 Mar.
When advance tax appliesSection 404: advance tax is payable when your tax for the year (after TDS credits) is Rs 10,000 or more. Missed instalments add interest under the Act.
TDS creditTax clients already deducted shows in your Form 26AS/AIS on the income tax portal and offsets your liability at filing.
Presumptive vs actualsIf your real expenses are high, regular taxation with books can beat the 50% scheme. Run both before deciding - a CA can help for the first year.
