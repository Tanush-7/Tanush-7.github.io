How to read this

Each drink decays on its own clock: remaining = dose × ½(hours since drink ÷ half-life). The chart adds all drinks together. The model assumes instant absorption — in reality caffeine peaks 30–60 minutes after a drink, so treat early peaks as approximate. Matcha and tea release caffeine more gently (L-theanine smooths the feel), but the milligrams still decay on the same half-life.

Caffeine per drink used here

  • Matcha (2 g whisked): ~68 mg · Black tea (240 ml): 47 mg · Masala chai (240 ml): ~50 mg · Green tea (240 ml): 28 mg

Typical averages compiled from FDA and USDA FoodData Central figures. Your actual cup varies with bean, leaf, dose and brew — edit any drink's mg in the list to match your cup.

Your drinking pattern

Life-expectancy loss from alcohol is about the habit, not one night. The strongest numbers come from Wood et al. (The Lancet, 2018): 599,912 drinkers in 19 countries.

8 / wk

The 100 g line

In Wood et al., all-cause mortality was lowest at 100 g of alcohol per week or less — about 7–8 beers (330 ml). Above it, life expectancy at age 40 fell in steps:

  • 100–200 g/week: about 6 months shorter
  • 200–350 g/week: about 1–2 years shorter
  • Over 350 g/week: about 4–5 years shorter

Two numbers, two different questions — read them apart

The band figure (e.g. “≈ 6 months shorter”) is Wood et al.'s estimate for everyone in that weekly band — the band's study average was ~123 g/week, so it describes the band, not your exact grams. The marginal figure answers a different question: what you save by cutting back to the line. The University of Cambridge summary of Wood et al. puts it pro-rata: above the low-risk level, each drink costs roughly 15–30 minutes of life expectancy — about the same as a cigarette. This calculator applies that range only to drinks above 100 g/week, so just over the line the marginal figure is small while the band figure is not. Both are population models, not a personal clock.

The per-cigarette figure was updated in December 2024 by University College London (Jackson et al., Addiction), using the British Doctors Study and the Million Women Study.

The pack maths

  • 1 cigarette: ~20 minutes of life expectancy
  • 1 pack of 20: ~6 hours 40 minutes — nearly 7 hours
  • 10 a day, quit on 1 Jan: you prevent the loss of a full day of life by 8 Jan (UCL's own example)
  • Lifelong smokers overall: lose about 10 years (men) to 11 years (women) vs non-smokers

Read this the right way

These are population averages for long-term smokers, spread across a lifetime of smoking — not a timer that starts when you light one cigarette. The harm is cumulative, and the same research is clear on the flip side: the sooner you stop, and the more cigarettes you don't smoke, the more life you keep. Quitting at any age recovers a large share of the loss.

Not medical advice. Every number here is a population average from the studies cited below, applied to the pattern you enter. Your personal half-life, risk and life expectancy depend on genetics, health, medicines and more. If you want to cut down or quit, a doctor can help with a plan that actually sticks.
