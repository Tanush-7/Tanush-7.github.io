Training plan

Current weekly structure: 3 runs, 2 lower-body and 2 upper-body sessions, with postural correction and core work built in. Morning sessions, 60–90 minutes. Sunday fully off. Tap a day to see the session.

Training notes

  • Running: distances are starting targets, not mandatory increases. Keep all three runs conversational and retain the 3-minute jog / 1-minute walk strategy.
  • Friday → Saturday: if Friday's leg session leaves you fatigued for the long run, reduce Friday's sets rather than compromising Saturday.
  • Effort: strength at RPE 7–8 (2–3 reps in reserve). Easy runs at a pace where full sentences are possible.
  • Daily targets: 2,000–2,100 kcal · 150–160 g protein · 8,000–12,000 steps.

Posture priorities

  • Anterior pelvic tilt: glute bridges, dead bugs, split squats, hip-flexor stretches.
  • Rounded shoulders: face pulls, reverse pec deck, chest-supported rows, external rotations.
  • Forward-head posture: chin tucks, wall slides, thoracic extensions.
  • Neck / upper-back: thoracic mobility, postural strengthening, upper-back work.
  • Abdominal prominence: overall fat loss, dead bugs, Pallof presses, side planks.
  • Flat feet: short-foot activation, single-leg balance, calf and tibialis raises.
  • Running mechanics: A-march, wall-lean march, hip-flexor strengthening, calf and soleus work.
  • V-taper: lat pulldowns, rows, lateral raises, upper-chest pressing.

Posture traits like pelvic tilt, rounded shoulders or a prominent base of the neck can't be diagnosed reliably from photos — several causes, including normal fat distribution, can look similar. Training improves strength and posture but can't guarantee a visible change. If anything is new, enlarging, painful, or comes with numbness, get it assessed.
